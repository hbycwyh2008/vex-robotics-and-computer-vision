# Core Meeting Playbook — Weeks 19–27

## Week 19 — Engineering Design and Gear Ratios

### Meeting 19A — Requirements, Constraints and Engineering Hypotheses
**Objective:** Students convert an open challenge into measurable requirements and write testable engineering hypotheses.

**Prep:** One simple mechanism challenge; design notebook; sample vague vs measurable requirements.

- **0–10 Launch:** Sort statements into requirement, constraint, preference or solution idea.
- **10–22 Learn:** Measurable requirement; constraint; criterion; `If → then → because` hypothesis.
- **22–40 Team planning:** Define 3–5 measurable requirements for the challenge.
- **40–58 Build baseline:** Create the simplest mechanism/configuration that can be tested.
- **58–70 Baseline test:** collect one meaningful performance measure.
- **70–80 Hypothesis:** choose one change and predict outcome.
- **80–90 Notebook/exit:** What evidence would falsify your hypothesis?

**Evidence:** requirement list + baseline + hypothesis.

### Meeting 19B — Gear Ratio: Speed vs Torque
**Anchor:** Lesson 13

**Objective:** Students explain driver/driven gears and use repeated tests to observe speed/torque trade-offs.

**Prep:** gear sets, motor, safe load or lift/test rig.

- **0–10 Predict:** Compare two gear arrangements; which should be faster/stronger?
- **10–22 Demo:** driver/driven gear, ratio, qualitative torque/speed trade-off.
- **22–48 Build/test:** teams test at least two ratios using same task/load.
- **48–62 Record:** time, success/failure and qualitative motor behavior.
- **62–72 Explain:** connect observed behavior to ratio.
- **72–82 Transfer:** choose ratio for a new scenario and defend.
- **82–90 Exit/reset:** fastest is not always best—give a mechanism-specific reason.

**Evidence:** gear-ratio comparison table.

## Week 20 — Mechanism Families and Prototype

### Meeting 20A — Mechanism Analysis: Intake, Lift, Gripper
**Objective:** Students identify function, force path, degrees of motion and failure risks in common mechanisms.

**Prep:** photos/real examples of simple VEX mechanisms; loose gears/shafts/structure.

- **0–10 Launch:** What job must a manipulator perform: capture, retain, move, release?
- **10–22 Mini-lesson:** mechanism as function + force path + constraints, not just shape.
- **22–42 Station analysis:** teams inspect examples and annotate input, output, supports and likely failure point.
- **42–55 Share:** groups compare different mechanisms for same task.
- **55–72 Sketch:** each team proposes two concepts with trade-offs.
- **72–82 Select:** decision matrix with 3 criteria.
- **82–90 Exit:** one reason the most complex design may be worse.

**Evidence:** two concepts + decision matrix.

### Meeting 20B — Manipulator Prototype Build
**Anchor:** Lesson 14

**Objective:** Students build a testable prototype focused on one required function.

**Prep:** mechanism parts; defined object; test criteria.

- **0–10 Requirement check:** restate one measurable function.
- **10–18 Build-risk demo:** shaft support, alignment, hard stops and pinch hazards.
- **18–55 Prototype:** build minimum viable mechanism.
- **55–68 First tests:** at least five attempts on same object/position.
- **68–76 Diagnose:** choose dominant failure mode.
- **76–84 One revision:** make a single justified change.
- **84–90 Exit:** what is your current bottleneck?

**Evidence:** prototype photo + baseline trial record.

## Week 21 — Rigidity, Metrics and Redesign

### Meeting 21A — Structural Reliability and Alignment
**Objective:** Students detect flex, misalignment and poor support and link those to measured mechanism inconsistency.

**Prep:** prototype mechanisms; inspection checklist.

- **0–10 Launch:** Observe a mechanism under load. Where does motion occur that was not intended?
- **10–20 Demo:** flex, bracing, shaft support, bearing alignment, fastener looseness.
- **20–45 Inspection:** teams mark intended vs unintended motion.
- **45–60 Controlled load tests:** same input/load repeated.
- **60–72 Redesign:** brace/support one identified weakness.
- **72–82 Retest:** compare variability/success.
- **82–90 Exit:** cite evidence that the structural change mattered—or did not.

**Evidence:** before/after test.

### Meeting 21B — Mechanism Metrics and Fair Testing
**Objective:** Students select metrics and control variables so mechanism comparisons are meaningful.

**Prep:** timer/ruler/objects; test-record template.

- **0–10 Sort:** good metric vs vague claim (“better”, “faster”, “more reliable”).
- **10–20 Learn:** independent/dependent/control variables at engineering level.
- **20–35 Test design:** define protocol and minimum trial count.
- **35–60 Baseline data:** collect repeated trials.
- **60–70 Summarize:** success rate/time/range as appropriate.
- **70–80 Peer audit:** is comparison fair and reproducible?
- **80–90 Exit:** identify one uncontrolled variable that could invalidate your conclusion.

**Evidence:** test protocol + dataset.

## Week 22 — Mechanism Challenge and Design Review

### Meeting 22A — Mechanism Challenge
**Objective:** Students improve performance through evidence-based iteration under constraints.

**Prep:** scoring rules; fixed test station; repair parts.

- **0–10 Requirements/scoring:** teams predict performance and failure risk.
- **10–25 Baseline:** official initial attempts.
- **25–45 Diagnose/build:** one major change only.
- **45–58 Retest:** same test protocol.
- **58–70 Second iteration if evidence supports it.
- **70–80 Final measured trials.
- **80–90 Notebook/exit:** strongest engineering decision and supporting evidence.

**Evidence:** baseline → change → retest chain.

### Meeting 22B — Design Review + Individual Defense
**Objective:** Students defend mechanism choices with evidence and respond to critique.

**Prep:** 3-minute presentation template; peer-review prompts.

- **0–10 Prepare:** requirement, design, data, failure, revision.
- **10–55 Reviews:** each team gives concise design review; peers ask one evidence question and one failure-mode question.
- **55–68 Individual spot-check:** random member explains gear ratio, support or test protocol.
- **68–78 Revision note:** what would you change next and why?
- **78–86 Portfolio:** commit photos/data/decision note.
- **86–90 Exit:** difference between “our design worked” and “our design is reliable.”

**Evidence:** design-review artifact + individual explanation.

## Week 23 — Competition-Style Strategy and Integration

### Meeting 23A — Strategy from Constraints and Scoring
**Objective:** Students choose a robot strategy by expected value/reliability rather than by maximum theoretical points.

**Prep:** simplified challenge/scoring system.

- **0–10 Launch:** Compare high-value low-reliability action with moderate-value high-reliability action.
- **10–22 Learn:** cycle time, risk, consistency, bottleneck and match/time budget.
- **22–40 Strategy planning:** create two candidate strategies.
- **40–58 Physical rehearsal:** driver/mechanism sequence at low speed.
- **58–70 Trial data:** measure completion/error.
- **70–80 Choose strategy:** justify with evidence.
- **80–90 Exit:** name one strategy choice changed by reliability data.

**Evidence:** strategy comparison.

### Meeting 23B — Driver + Mechanism Integration
**Objective:** Students integrate drivetrain and manipulator while identifying interface failures.

**Prep:** challenge field; charged batteries.

- **0–10 Preflight:** mechanical/electrical/configuration check.
- **10–35 Subsystem warm-up:** test drive and mechanism separately.
- **35–60 Integrated trials:** record first failure point.
- **60–72 Diagnose interface:** driver timing, code mode, mechanism, wiring or field interaction.
- **72–82 One change/retest.
- **82–90 Exit:** why subsystem success does not guarantee integrated success.

**Evidence:** integration failure log.

- **68–78 Transfer:** new object/constraint; propose justified design change.
- **78–86 Learning log.
- **86–90 Reset/reassessment targets.

## Week 25 — Computer Vision: Images as Data

### Meeting 25A — What a Camera Actually Gives the Computer
**Anchor:** Lesson 17

**Objective:** Students explain images as numerical arrays and inspect dimensions, channels and pixel values with Python/OpenCV.

**Prep:** Python/OpenCV environment; sample images; IBM Coursera access; `cv-coursera-viewing-guide.md`.

- **0–8 Launch:** Show an image and ask: “What does the computer literally receive?”
- **8–18 Video:** IBM — *Introduction to Computer Vision* + brief discussion.
- **18–28 Demo:** load image, print shape/dtype, inspect one pixel.
- **28–55 Lab:** students load image, locate coordinates, inspect 5+ pixels.
- **55–68 Rebuild:** close example and recreate the minimum pipeline from checklist.
- **68–80 Explain:** width/height/channel and top-left coordinate system.
- **80–90 Evidence/exit:** screenshot + answer: camera gives data, not semantic understanding.

**Evidence:** first OpenCV script/notebook.

### Meeting 25B — RGB/BGR, Channels and Regions of Interest
**Objective:** Students manipulate channels and regions and explain what information is preserved/lost.

**Prep:** IBM Module 2 videos *What Is A Digital Image* and *Manipulating Images One Pixel At a Time*.

- **0–10 Retrieval:** interpret shape `(height, width, channels)`.
- **10–23 Video/notes:** selected IBM segments.
- **23–35 Demo:** OpenCV BGR vs RGB; grayscale; channel extraction.
- **35–60 Lab:** isolate channels, crop ROI, compare numeric values.
- **60–72 Failure case:** incorrect color display from BGR/RGB mismatch; students diagnose.
- **72–82 Share:** what changed and why.
- **82–90 Exit:** explain BGR/RGB mismatch without using “OpenCV is weird” as an answer.

**Evidence:** annotated channel/ROI output.

## Week 26 — HSV, Thresholding and Masks

### Meeting 26A — Why HSV for Color Rules
**Anchor:** Lesson 18

**Objective:** Students sample HSV values and derive—not guess—initial color thresholds.

**Prep:** colored targets; varied backgrounds; OpenCV HSV documentation; IBM image-processing review.

- **0–10 Launch:** same colored object under brighter/dimmer light; which RGB values change?
- **10–20 Learn/demo:** hue, saturation, value; BGR→HSV conversion.
- **20–45 Sampling lab:** collect target and background HSV values.
- **45–58 Threshold proposal:** choose lower/upper bounds from data.
- **58–72 Build mask:** apply `inRange` or equivalent.
- **72–82 Inspect errors:** find target pixels missed/background included.
- **82–90 Exit:** why wider range can reduce false negatives but increase false positives.

**Evidence:** HSV sample table + first mask.

### Meeting 26B — Mask Testing Under Changing Conditions
**Objective:** Students quantify threshold brittleness across lighting/background/distance.

**Prep:** 3–4 controlled conditions; test matrix.

- **0–10 Predict:** rank conditions from easiest to hardest.
- **10–18 Test protocol:** change one condition when possible.
- **18–50 Trials:** run same thresholds across conditions.
- **50–62 Label errors:** FP/FN at frame or target level.
- **62–74 Tune one bound:** predict effect first.
- **74–82 Retest same cases.
- **82–90 Exit:** report a trade-off rather than claiming “fixed.”

**Evidence:** condition matrix + before/after threshold.

## Week 27 — Morphology, Calibration and Robustness

### Meeting 27A — Morphological Cleanup: Solve a Specific Problem
**Objective:** Students use erosion/dilation/opening/closing only when a mask failure justifies it.

**Prep:** masks with speckle noise and holes; OpenCV morphology reference.

- **0–10 Launch:** compare two masks; identify exact defect.
- **10–22 Demo:** erosion/dilation concept with small visual examples; opening/closing as combinations.
- **22–45 Lab:** test one operation/kernel on same image.
- **45–58 Compare:** count/observe noise removal and target damage.
- **58–70 Choose:** apply only operation that solves measured defect.
- **70–82 Rebuild:** students recreate pipeline and explain each stage.
- **82–90 Exit:** “Why is more image processing not automatically better?”

**Evidence:** before/after mask + justification.

### Meeting 27B — Calibration Protocol + Robustness Experiment
**Objective:** Students create a repeatable calibration procedure and define when recalibration is needed.

**Prep:** multiple lighting/background setups; calibration worksheet.

- **0–10 Retrieval:** list variables that shift color perception.
- **10–20 Learn:** calibration is a procedure, not one magic threshold.
- **20–38 Design protocol:** sampling points, target/background, lighting notes, saved ranges.
- **38–60 Execute on Setup A and B.
- **60–72 Compare:** one threshold set vs recalibrated sets.
- **72–82 Decision:** when should system recalibrate vs tolerate variation?
- **82–90 Exit:** write a 4-step calibration procedure another team could reproduce.

**Evidence:** calibration protocol + comparison.
