# Mastery Levels — Robotics and Engineering

This VEX Robotics course uses the same **Mastery Level 0–5** language as the Full-Stack Web and AI course so students experience one consistent learning culture across CS and engineering courses.

> Working once is not mastery. Real mastery means you can explain, rebuild, debug, modify, and transfer a skill.

## Level 0 — Exposure

You have seen the concept, robot behavior, build step, code pattern, or teacher demonstration, but you cannot yet reproduce or explain it reliably.

**Robotics example:** You watched a drivetrain demonstration but cannot identify the major parts or reproduce the setup.

## Level 1 — Follow and Make It Work

You can follow instructions, a build guide, or a provided code example and make the system work.

**Robotics example:** You follow the official TrainingBot instructions and complete a drivetrain that moves.

## Level 2 — Explain While Looking

You can look at the robot, code, wiring diagram, notebook, or test setup and explain what the important parts do and why they matter.

**Robotics example:** You can explain how the motors, gears, wheels, controller configuration, and code work together to drive the robot.

## Level 3 — Rebuild with a Checklist

You can recreate the same pattern with a short checklist or engineering notebook entry, without copying the complete solution.

**Robotics example:** Given a checklist, you can rebuild or reconfigure a drivetrain, sensor routine, or test procedure and make it work again.

## Level 4 — Rebuild Independently

You can reproduce the core pattern from a blank starting point without full instructions, solution code, or step-by-step teacher help.

**Robotics example:** You can independently configure the robot, write the required control logic, run a valid test, and document the result.

## Level 5 — Modify, Debug, and Transfer

You can diagnose failure, use evidence to improve the system, modify the pattern for a new constraint, and transfer the idea to a new engineering challenge.

**Robotics example:** The robot turns inconsistently. You collect trial data, form a hypothesis, modify hardware or control parameters, retest, justify the change, and apply the improved method to a different autonomous challenge.

---

## What Mastery Measures

Mastery Level answers:

> **How independently can I perform, explain, rebuild, debug, modify, and transfer this skill?**

It is different from the project rubric. A project rubric answers:

> **How well did this particular project meet the engineering requirements?**

A student can therefore have a high mastery level in a particular skill while a team project still receives a lower rubric score because of reliability, documentation, design quality, or incomplete requirements.

---

## Evidence of Mastery in VEX

Depending on the lesson, mastery evidence may include:

- a working robot or subsystem;
- code or device configuration;
- an engineering notebook entry;
- repeated test data;
- a debugging log;
- a design sketch or wiring diagram;
- GitHub commits or portfolio evidence;
- an oral technical explanation;
- a rebuild or transfer challenge.

The engineering notebook is not proof by itself. It must contain evidence that connects decisions to real tests and observations.

---

## Course Expectation

Students are expected to move progressively from **following** toward **independent engineering**.

For major course skills, the typical long-term target is **Level 3–4**. Capstone work should provide opportunities for **Level 4–5** performance.
