# Unit Quiz Bank

Use short quizzes selectively. The course is performance-heavy; quizzes should check concepts students need to make engineering decisions.

## Unit 00–01 — VEX Foundations / TrainingBot
1. What is the functional difference between a shaft, spacer and shaft collar?
2. Why should a robot be powered down before rewiring devices?
3. Why is a build guide easier to debug when teams identify the last known-correct step?
4. Give one sign of poor drivetrain alignment.
5. Why should motor ports be documented?

## Unit 02 — Driver Control and Programming
1. What must match between the physical robot and VEXcode device configuration?
2. What problem does a joystick deadband solve?
3. Compare tank drive and arcade drive.
4. Why are functions useful in robot-control code?
5. A robot drives forward but curves right. List one mechanical and one software hypothesis.
6. Why is one successful run insufficient evidence of reliability?

## Unit 03 — Sensors and Autonomous Control
1. What is the difference between raw sensor data and a threshold decision?
2. Define false positive and false negative in a sensor/vision context.
3. Compare open-loop and feedback control.
4. Why might a robot overshoot a 90° target heading?
5. What is a state transition?
6. Why should autonomous trials use a consistent start position?

## Unit 04 — Engineering Design and Mechanisms
1. Identify driver and driven gear in a simple gear pair.
2. Explain the speed/torque trade-off in gear reduction.
3. Why is shaft support important?
4. Write a valid engineering hypothesis using an if–then–because structure.
5. Why should teams change one major variable at a time?
6. Explain why the fastest mechanism may not be the best mechanism.

## Unit 05 — Computer Vision
1. What data structure does a digital image represent conceptually?
2. What do H, S and V represent in HSV?
3. What does a binary color mask classify?
4. What is a contour?
5. What information can be extracted from a bounding box?
6. Give one condition that could cause a false color detection.

## Unit 06 — AI Robotics Integration
1. Write the three main layers in the course architecture.
2. Why should perception, decision and action be separated in code?
3. What is the purpose of a center tolerance band?
4. Why might a vision-guided robot oscillate?
5. Give two safe fallback behaviors when a target is lost.
6. Why should a system be stress-tested under non-ideal conditions?

## Unit 07 — Capstone
1. What makes a requirement measurable?
2. What is requirement traceability?
3. Why should subsystems be tested before full integration?
4. Explain the difference between a limitation and a bug.
5. What evidence is needed to claim a system is reliable?
6. What should an AI-use disclosure include?

## Scoring Guidance
Prefer short explanations over memorized definitions. A strong response should connect vocabulary to a physical/programming example.
