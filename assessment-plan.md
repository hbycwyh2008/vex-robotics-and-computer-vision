# Assessment Plan

This course uses portfolio-based, project-centered assessment with frequent low-stakes checks. The goal is to assess **engineering thinking** — building, debugging, iterating, documenting and defending decisions — not just final results.

The assessment system deliberately separates two questions:

1. **Mastery:** How independently can the student perform, explain, rebuild, debug, modify, and transfer a skill?
2. **Project quality:** How well does a particular engineering project meet its requirements?

These are related, but they are not the same measure.

---

## Mastery Levels 0–5

The course uses the shared Mastery Level system:

| Mastery Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Exposure — has seen the skill or concept |
| 1 | Follow and make it work |
| 2 | Explain while looking |
| 3 | Rebuild with a checklist |
| 4 | Rebuild independently |
| 5 | Modify, debug, and transfer |

Mastery Levels are used for learning targets, evidence checks, oral explanations, rebuild tasks, and capstone skill verification.

See [mastery-levels.md](mastery-levels.md).

> **Mastery Level is not a replacement for the 4-level project rubric.** A student may independently demonstrate a skill at Mastery Level 4 while a team project receives a project-rubric score of 3 because of reliability, documentation, design quality, or incomplete requirements.

## Evidence Sources

### Team Engineering Notebook

Shows the team's shared engineering process:

> **Goal → Design / Change → Test → Problem → Diagnosis → Decision → Retest → Next Step**

Use:

- [Engineering Notebook Guide](student-handouts/engineering-notebook-guide.md)
- [Team Engineering Notebook Template](student-handouts/engineering-notebook-template.md)

### Individual Engineering Learning Log

Shows each student's personal contribution, understanding, and current mastery. It helps prevent a successful team result from being treated automatically as evidence that every team member independently understands the work.

Use [Individual Engineering Learning Log](student-handouts/individual-engineering-learning-log.md).

### Test Evidence

When a claim involves reliability, accuracy, consistency, or improvement, students should use repeated trials or controlled comparisons.

Use [Engineering Test Record Template](student-handouts/test-record-template.md) when useful.

### GitHub / Markdown Portfolio

Curates major evidence from the course: code, photos/video, engineering decisions, debugging stories, test results, iteration, reflection, and AI-use disclosure.

## Assessment Types

### 1. Formative Checks — Ongoing

Quick, low-stakes checks during lessons:

- exit tickets;
- predictions;
- debugging logs;
- mini-demos;
- short concept questions;
- oral technical explanations;
- short rebuild checks;
- individual learning-log entries.

These are used to identify misunderstanding and determine whether students are ready for reduced scaffolding.

See [assessments/formative-checks.md](assessments/formative-checks.md).

### 2. Unit Quizzes

Short concept checks at the end of major phases. These focus on vocabulary, core ideas, diagram/code interpretation and engineering reasoning rather than memorization.

See [assessments/unit-quizzes.md](assessments/unit-quizzes.md).

### 3. Project Assessments

The core of the grade. Each major performance milestone is assessed using one or more aligned rubrics.

| Project / Evidence | Primary rubric |
|---|---|
| Team Engineering Notebook & portfolio | [Engineering Notebook Rubric](rubrics/engineering-notebook-rubric.md) |
| Robot builds / mechanisms | [Robot Build Rubric](rubrics/robot-build-rubric.md) |
| Driver control / autonomous programming | [Robot Programming Rubric](rubrics/robot-programming-rubric.md) |
| Computer vision projects | [Computer Vision Project Rubric](rubrics/computer-vision-project-rubric.md) |
| Final integrated project | [Final Project Rubric](rubrics/final-project-rubric.md) |
| Presentations / engineering defense | [Presentation Rubric](rubrics/presentation-rubric.md) |

Individual Learning Logs, oral explanations, and rebuild checks may be used to verify that each student personally understands the team's submitted work.

See [assessments/project-assessments.md](assessments/project-assessments.md).

### 4. Final Assessment

The integrated robotics + AI vision capstone, its portfolio documentation, repeated validation and engineering defense.

Capstone assessment should include both:

- **project quality** using the existing project rubrics; and
- **individual mastery evidence** through explanation, rebuild, debugging, or transfer questions.

See [assessments/final-assessment.md](assessments/final-assessment.md).

## What Every Major Rubric Values

Across projects, students are expected to demonstrate:

- technical correctness;
- safe, reliable implementation;
- systematic debugging;
- repeated testing;
- evidence-based iteration;
- documentation quality;
- ability to explain code/design;
- honest limitation analysis;
- responsible AI use;
- team collaboration where applicable.

## 4-Level Project Rubric Scale

This existing project-quality scale remains in place:

| Level | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Exceeds Expectations | Independent, well-tested, clearly defended, goes beyond requirements |
| 3 | Meets Expectations | Meets requirements competently with sufficient evidence |
| 2 | Approaching Expectations | Partially meets requirements; evidence or understanding has gaps |
| 1 | Beginning | Early progress; major technical/evidence gaps remain |

Do not relabel this scale as Mastery Levels. The two systems serve different purposes.

## Suggested Grade Weighting

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Engineering notebook, individual evidence & portfolio | 20% |
| Formative checks & quizzes | 15% |
| Projects / engineering challenges | 40% |
| Final integrated project | 20% |
| Presentation & reflection | 5% |

> Adjust to school policy. Preserve enough weight on process so students are rewarded for debugging, iteration and evidence rather than a lucky final demo.

Within the 20% engineering-evidence category, teachers may combine Team Engineering Notebook evidence with individual logs, mastery checks, and portfolio quality according to local grading policy.

## Academic Integrity and AI Use

Students may use AI tools when allowed, but they must **disclose, understand and verify** meaningful AI contributions. AI-generated code or explanations do not count as evidence that a robot/vision system works.

Students must provide real measurements, test results and direct system evidence.

## Assessment Principle

A polished final robot is not sufficient on its own. A student who can show a thoughtful failed test, diagnose the cause, improve the system, retest it, and explain the evidence may demonstrate stronger engineering learning than a student whose robot happened to work without documented reasoning.

Likewise, a strong team result is not sufficient evidence that every team member has the same mastery level. Individual explanation, rebuilding, debugging, and transfer are valid ways to verify personal learning.
