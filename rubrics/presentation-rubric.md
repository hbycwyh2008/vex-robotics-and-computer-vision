# Technical Presentation & Engineering Defense Rubric

| Dimension | 4 — Exceeds | 3 — Meets | 2 — Approaching | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem/context | Problem and constraints are concise and compelling | Problem is clearly stated | Some context missing | Problem unclear |
| System explanation | Clearly explains structure + control + perception + decisions | Explains major subsystems | Explanation incomplete | Cannot explain system |
| Evidence | Uses measured results to support claims | Includes relevant test evidence | Limited evidence | Claims unsupported |
| Failure/limitations | Specific failures and limits are analyzed honestly | At least one meaningful limitation explained | Vague limitations | Presents system as flawless |
| Engineering trade-offs | Defends decisions using constraints and data | Major design choices justified | Some choices unexplained | Decisions arbitrary |
| Communication | Clear, organized, appropriate visuals, shared team participation | Understandable and organized | Uneven clarity or participation | Difficult to follow |
| Q&A / defense | Answers technical questions with evidence and reasoning | Answers core questions accurately | Needs significant prompting | Cannot defend work |
| AI disclosure | Clearly explains where AI assisted and how output was verified | Appropriate disclosure included | Disclosure incomplete | AI contribution hidden/unexplained |

## Presentation guidance
A strong presentation prioritizes:
1. problem and requirements;
2. architecture;
3. evidence from testing;
4. major iteration;
5. limitations;
6. live/demo evidence;
7. next steps.

Do not spend most of the presentation reading code line-by-line.
