# Lesson 02 — TrainingBot Build I: Chassis and Drivetrain

## Course position
Weeks 3–4 · VEX V5 Foundations and TrainingBot

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- follow an official VEX build sequence accurately;
- identify the chassis and drivetrain as functional subsystems;
- use correct fasteners, spacers, shafts and structural components;
- apply a build-check-debug cycle instead of guessing;
- document deviations and corrections in an engineering notebook.

## Required resources
- one VEX V5 Competition Starter Kit per build team
- official VEX V5 TrainingBot build instructions
- correct hex keys and nut-driving tools
- parts trays
- engineering notebook / portfolio

## Teacher framing
TrainingBot is the baseline training platform for this course. Students should first reproduce a known working design before modifying mechanisms or writing advanced autonomous code.

## Build protocol
For every major step:
1. **Read** the complete step before taking parts.
2. **Identify** every required component.
3. **Orient** the structure exactly as shown.
4. **Assemble** without fully tightening uncertain connections.
5. **Compare** the physical build with the diagram.
6. **Test** free movement where applicable.
7. **Document** any mistake that required disassembly.

## Hands-on mission
Build the first major portion of TrainingBot, prioritizing:
- chassis structure;
- left/right symmetry;
- drivetrain alignment;
- shafts, bearings and spacers;
- wheel clearance;
- motor mounting if reached during the available class period.

The objective is **correctness**, not finishing first.

## Engineering checks
Before continuing, teams inspect:
- Are the left and right sides symmetric where intended?
- Are shafts supported and able to rotate correctly?
- Are spacers/collars located so parts cannot slide excessively?
- Are gears or wheels rubbing against structure?
- Are screws secure without deforming parts or stripping heads?
- Does the chassis sit squarely on the work surface?

## Debugging protocol
If the build does not match the guide:
- find the last step that is definitely correct;
- compare orientation and hole positions;
- identify the smallest section that must be rebuilt;
- change one thing at a time;
- record the cause, not only the fix.

## Evidence to submit
- 2–4 progress photos from different stages;
- one photo showing a drivetrain detail;
- notebook entry describing one assembly error or near-error and how it was detected;
- team member responsibilities for the lesson.

## Team roles
Rotate roles rather than allowing one experienced student to build everything:
- Builder
- Parts / diagram verifier
- Quality-control inspector
- Documentation lead

With two students, combine Builder + documentation and Verifier + quality control, then switch halfway.

## AI use
AI can explain mechanical vocabulary or help reason about why a mechanism binds. It cannot reliably inspect a build that students have not compared against the official instructions. Official build documentation remains the source of truth for TrainingBot assembly.

## Exit check
Explain the difference between these three functions:
- a **shaft** transmits rotation;
- a **spacer** establishes separation/position;
- a **shaft collar** constrains axial movement.

## Next lesson
Complete TrainingBot, connect the V5 electronics, perform a mechanical inspection, and prepare for first power-on.
