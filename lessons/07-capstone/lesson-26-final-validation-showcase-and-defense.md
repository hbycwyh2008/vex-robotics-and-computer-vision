# Lesson 26 — Final Validation, Showcase and Engineering Defense

## Course position
Week 36 · Integrated Robotics + AI Vision Capstone

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- run a final validation protocol under documented conditions;
- compare final performance with stated requirements;
- demonstrate the integrated robot safely and repeatably;
- explain the complete perception–decision–action architecture;
- defend engineering choices, test evidence, AI use and known limitations.

## Final validation
Before the public/class demonstration, run a defined final test set.

At minimum include:
- repeated normal-condition trials;
- one changed-environment or stress-test condition;
- verification of safe fallback behavior;
- a final hardware inspection;
- a final software/version check.

Do not make untested last-minute changes immediately before the showcase.

## Final evidence table
For each requirement from the proposal, report:
- requirement;
- test method;
- result;
- evidence location;
- status: met / partially met / not met.

## Demonstration structure
Each team should show:
1. the problem being solved;
2. the robot/mechanism;
3. the vision/perception output;
4. the decision logic;
5. the resulting robot action;
6. at least one complete task attempt;
7. fallback or failure behavior if appropriate.

## Engineering defense
Students answer questions such as:
- Why did you choose this mechanism?
- Which sensor/vision feature drives the main decision?
- What is the most important threshold or rule?
- What did repeated testing reveal?
- Which failure mode remains most serious?
- What evidence shows the final system improved over the prototype?
- Where did AI tools assist, and how did you verify their suggestions?
- What would you change with two more weeks?

## Required final portfolio
Include:
- final problem statement and architecture diagram;
- build photos and hardware configuration;
- code/repository link;
- vision pipeline explanation;
- decision/state diagram;
- requirement traceability table;
- test data and charts/tables where useful;
- debugging/iteration evidence;
- final demonstration video or equivalent evidence;
- limitations and future work;
- AI-assistance disclosure.

## Assessment dimensions
Suggested dimensions:
- mechanical reliability;
- software/control quality;
- vision/perception quality;
- integration quality;
- testing and evidence;
- engineering reasoning;
- documentation;
- presentation/defense;
- responsible AI use.

## Reflection
Students answer:
1. What part of the system became more difficult when software had to control real hardware?
2. What did the camera/sensors perceive that humans tend to take for granted?
3. Which test changed your design the most?
4. What is one claim about your system that the evidence does **not** support?
5. What robotics/AI topic would you learn next?

## Course synthesis
Students should leave the course understanding that an intelligent robot is not a single algorithm. It is a system of:

**Structure + Power + Actuation + Sensors/Vision + Software + Decision Logic + Testing + Human Engineering Judgment**

The final goal is not a perfect demo. It is a system students can build, explain, test, improve and defend with evidence.
