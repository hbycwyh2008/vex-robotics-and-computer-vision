# Lesson 01 — Course Orientation, Safety, and the VEX V5 Kit

## Course Position

Week 1 · Unit 00 — Getting Started  
**Recommended duration:** 90 minutes

This is the first course meeting. Students should leave understanding both **how this course works** and how to safely begin working with VEX hardware.

---

## Shared Course Learning Flow

Introduce the same learning language used across the CS program:

> **Learn → Practice → Rebuild → Share**

Then connect it to the VEX engineering cycle:

> **Predict → Build / Program → Test → Debug → Redesign → Retest → Document → Explain / Transfer**

Show the local classroom posters in [`../../public-documents/posters/`](../../public-documents/posters/README.md).

Core message for students:

> A robot working once is not the end of learning. You should become able to explain it, rebuild important patterns, debug failures, improve the system, and defend decisions with evidence.

---

## Learning Objectives

Students will be able to:

- explain the shared course flow: **Learn → Practice → Rebuild → Share**;
- describe the purpose of Mastery Levels 0–5;
- explain the difference between a working team robot and individual mastery;
- identify the major subsystems in the VEX V5 Competition Starter Kit;
- distinguish structure, motion, electronics, fasteners, shafts, gears, spacers, and collars;
- explain safe handling of batteries, motors, shafts, tools, and metal structure;
- organize parts so a build can be assembled, debugged, and restored efficiently;
- begin a **Team Engineering Notebook** with photographic and written evidence;
- complete a first **Individual Engineering Learning Log** reflection.

---

## Mastery Target

**Target for Lesson 01: Level 2 — Explain While Looking**

By the end of class, students should be able to look at the posters, VEX kit, and first notebook evidence and explain:

- how learning works in this course;
- what the major VEX subsystems do;
- why engineering work must be tested and documented.

Level 3 rebuilding is introduced as a future expectation but is not required on the first day.

See [`../../mastery-levels.md`](../../mastery-levels.md).

## 0–15 min — Course Orientation / Poster Walk

Do not begin with a long syllabus lecture.

Students briefly inspect the four posters. In pairs or small groups, answer:

1. What is the main message of this poster?
2. What does it mean for you in a robotics class?
3. What is one example of a student behavior that would match the poster?

### Required debrief questions

- If a team follows the TrainingBot instructions and the robot works, what Mastery Level might that initially demonstrate?
- What would a student have to do to demonstrate stronger mastery?
- Why is **rebuild** part of the classroom flow?
- How could AI help with robotics without replacing engineering thinking?

Teacher should establish two course rules clearly:

> **Working once is not mastery.**

> **A team result does not automatically prove individual mastery.**

---

## 15–20 min — Launch Question

A robot fails during a challenge.

Which is easier to debug:

- a robot whose parts, wiring, code, tests, failures, and changes are documented; or
- one built without a record?

Why?

Connect student answers to the Team Engineering Notebook.

---

## 20–30 min — Mini Lesson: Think in Subsystems

Use five categories when examining the kit:

1. **Structure** — channels, angles, plates and fasteners.
2. **Motion** — wheels, gears, sprockets, shafts, bearings and spacers.
3. **Actuation** — V5 Smart Motors.
4. **Control** — V5 Brain, Controller and Smart Cables.
5. **Sensing / intelligence** — sensors and later computer-vision components.

The goal is not to memorize every part number. Students should learn what each family of parts *does* in a robot.

---

## 30–65 min — Guided Practice: Kit Investigation

### Part A — Inventory Walk

Teams open the kit and sort representative components into the five subsystem categories.

For at least **12 different components**, record:

- component name or best identification;
- category;
- what job it performs;
- what it connects to;
- one handling or assembly note.

### Part B — Hardware Identification Challenge

Teacher displays or calls out examples such as:

- shaft collar;
- spacer;
- shaft;
- gear;
- bearing;
- C-channel;
- L-channel / angle;
- screw, nut and nut retainer;
- Smart Motor;
- Brain;
- Controller.

Students locate the component and explain its likely role.

### Part C — Storage System

Teams establish a repeatable storage system. Similar-looking parts that perform different functions should not be mixed simply because they look alike.

---

## Safety Baseline

- Power the robot down before changing wiring or mechanisms.
- Do not short, puncture, crush, or modify batteries.
- Keep fingers, hair, clothing and cables away from moving mechanisms.
- Do not force screws or overtighten fasteners.
- Treat cut metal edges and protruding shafts as hazards.
- Use the correct tool size; stop if a fastener begins to strip.
- Keep the work area clear enough to find dropped hardware.
- Never test a mechanism in a way that places a person in the expected path of motion.

---

## 65–78 min — Team Engineering Notebook: First Entry

Students create the first notebook entry.

Minimum required evidence:

- **Date / lesson / team members**
- **Today's goal:** safely understand and organize the VEX kit
- one labeled photo of the organized kit;
- subsystem table with at least 12 components;
- one observation about a part that was easy to confuse;
- one safety or organization decision the team made;
- one next step for the TrainingBot build.

Introduce the recurring notebook pattern:

> **Goal → Design / Change → Test → Problem → Diagnosis → Decision → Retest → Next Step**

Explain that not every first-day entry needs every stage, but future engineering sessions will increasingly use the full cycle.

See:

- [Engineering Notebook Guide](../../student-handouts/engineering-notebook-guide.md)
- [Team Engineering Notebook Template](../../student-handouts/engineering-notebook-template.md)

## 78–85 min — Individual Engineering Learning Log

Each student completes a short individual entry.

Required prompts for Day 1:

1. One VEX component or subsystem I can now explain:
2. One safety or organization decision I understand:
3. One thing I personally contributed to the team today:
4. My current Mastery Level for VEX kit identification and why:
5. One thing I still need to learn before I could rebuild/configure a robot independently:

Use [Individual Engineering Learning Log](../../student-handouts/individual-engineering-learning-log.md).

## 85–90 min — Reset + Exit Mastery Check

### Physical reset

- return tools and loose parts;
- confirm battery and electronics are stored safely;
- save notebook evidence;
- leave the workspace ready for the next class.

### Exit questions

Students answer individually:

1. Why does this course include **rebuild** after guided practice?
2. What is the difference between **team project success** and **individual mastery**?
3. Name one VEX subsystem and explain what it does.

---

## Evidence to Submit

### Team evidence

1. One labeled photo of the organized kit.
2. A subsystem table containing at least 12 parts.
3. First Team Engineering Notebook entry.

### Individual evidence

4. First Individual Engineering Learning Log entry.
5. Exit mastery check.

---

## AI Use

AI may help students turn observations into clearer explanations, but it may not replace physical identification.

Students must verify every AI-generated part name against the actual component and VEX documentation.

AI may not invent:

- component observations;
- measurements;
- test results;
- evidence of work not actually completed.

Refer to the [AI Use Policy poster](../../public-documents/posters/ai-use-policy.svg).

---

## Teacher Notes

- Do not rush into assembly. A deliberate first lesson reduces lost hardware, stripped fasteners, poor documentation habits, and confusion later.
- The course-orientation block should be active and brief; do not read the posters aloud line by line.
- Photograph the original storage layout before students reorganize anything.
- Reinforce that Team Engineering Notebook evidence and Individual Learning Log evidence serve different purposes.
- Beginning with Mastery Level 2 is intentional. Students have not yet had enough repeated practice to demonstrate Level 3–5.
