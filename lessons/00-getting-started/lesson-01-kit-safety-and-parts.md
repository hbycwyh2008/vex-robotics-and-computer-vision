# Lesson 01 — Meet the VEX V5 Competition Starter Kit

## Course position
Week 1 · Robotics Foundations

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- identify the major subsystems in the VEX V5 Competition Starter Kit;
- distinguish structure, motion, electronics, fasteners, shafts, gears, spacers, and collars;
- explain safe handling of batteries, motors, shafts, tools, and metal structure;
- organize parts so a build can be assembled, debugged, and restored efficiently;
- begin an engineering notebook with photographic and written evidence.

## Required resources
- VEX V5 Competition Starter Kit
- V5 Brain, Controller, Battery, Smart Motors and Smart Cables
- kit hardware and structure
- hex keys / nut drivers appropriate for VEX hardware
- labeled storage trays or bins
- engineering notebook or digital portfolio

## Launch question
A robot fails during a challenge. Which is easier to debug: a robot whose parts, wiring, code, and changes are documented, or one built without a system? Why?

## Mini lesson — Think in subsystems
Use five categories when examining the kit:
1. **Structure** — channels, angles, plates and fasteners.
2. **Motion** — wheels, gears, sprockets, shafts, bearings and spacers.
3. **Actuation** — V5 Smart Motors.
4. **Control** — V5 Brain, Controller and Smart Cables.
5. **Sensing / intelligence** — sensors and later computer-vision components.

The goal is not to memorize every part number. Students should learn what each family of parts *does* in a robot.

## Hands-on mission
### Part A — Inventory walk
Teams open the kit and sort representative components into the five subsystem categories.

For at least 12 different components, record:
- component name or best identification;
- category;
- what job it performs;
- what it connects to;
- one handling or assembly note.

### Part B — Hardware identification challenge
Teacher displays or calls out examples such as:
- shaft collar;
- spacer;
- shaft;
- gear;
- bearing;
- C-channel;
- L-channel / angle;
- screw, nut and nut retainer;
- Smart Motor;
- Brain;
- Controller.

Students locate the component and explain its likely role.

### Part C — Storage system
Teams establish a repeatable storage system. Similar-looking parts that perform different functions should not be mixed simply because they look alike.

## Safety baseline
- Power the robot down before changing wiring or mechanisms.
- Do not short, puncture, crush, or modify batteries.
- Keep fingers, hair, clothing and cables away from moving mechanisms.
- Do not force screws or overtighten fasteners.
- Treat cut metal edges and protruding shafts as hazards.
- Use the correct tool size; stop if a fastener begins to strip.
- Keep the work area clear enough to find dropped hardware.

## Evidence to submit
1. One labeled photo of the organized kit.
2. A subsystem table containing at least 12 parts.
3. A short engineering-notebook entry: **What three parts were easiest to confuse, and how will you distinguish them next time?**

## Mastery check
A student is ready to continue when they can independently locate and explain the purpose of the Brain, Controller, Smart Motor, shaft, spacer, shaft collar, gear, structural channel, screw and nut.

## AI use
AI may help students turn observations into clearer explanations, but it may not replace physical identification. Students must verify every AI-generated part name against the actual component and VEX documentation.

## Teacher notes
Do not rush into assembly. A deliberate first lesson reduces lost hardware, stripped fasteners and confusion later. Photograph the original storage layout before students reorganize anything.
