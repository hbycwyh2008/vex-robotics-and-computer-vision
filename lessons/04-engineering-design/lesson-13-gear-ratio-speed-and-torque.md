# Lesson 13 — Gear Ratio: Speed and Torque Trade-Offs

## Course position
Weeks 19–20 · Engineering Design and Mechanisms

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- identify driver and driven gears in a simple gear train;
- calculate and interpret a basic gear ratio;
- predict whether a configuration favors speed or torque;
- test predictions on a physical mechanism;
- connect mathematical ratio to observed robot performance.

## Launch question
Why can the same motor make one mechanism move quickly and another move slowly but with more force?

## Core idea
A gear train changes the relationship between motor rotation and mechanism rotation. In this course, students use gear ratio as an engineering decision rather than only a calculation exercise.

For a simple pair, students should consistently identify:
- driver gear;
- driven gear;
- tooth counts;
- expected direction;
- expected speed/torque trade-off.

## Hands-on investigation
Build or configure at least two simple gear arrangements with different ratios.

For each arrangement:
1. predict relative output speed;
2. predict relative torque capability;
3. run the same motor command;
4. measure or compare output behavior;
5. explain whether the observation matches the prediction.

## Suggested tests
Depending on available hardware:
- rotations completed in a fixed time;
- time to rotate a mechanism through a fixed angle;
- ability to lift/move the same safe test load;
- ability to start from rest under load.

Do not use unsafe loads or stall motors deliberately for extended periods.

## Engineering table
Record:
- driver teeth;
- driven teeth;
- ratio representation;
- predicted effect;
- measured observation;
- preferred use case.

## Design decision challenge
Choose a gear ratio for one scenario and defend it:
- fast intake roller;
- lifting arm;
- drivetrain requiring more pushing force;
- mechanism requiring precise slow motion.

## Evidence to submit
- labeled gear diagram;
- ratio calculations;
- test data for two configurations;
- one recommendation supported by evidence.

## AI use
AI may check a ratio calculation or explain terminology, but students must identify the actual driver/driven gears and verify performance on the real mechanism.

## Extension
Investigate compound gear trains or compare motor cartridge/gearing options if the available V5 hardware supports the activity.

## Next lesson
Apply gear-ratio thinking to a real manipulator mechanism such as an intake, arm, lift or pusher.
