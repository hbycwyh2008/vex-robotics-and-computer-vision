# Lesson 16 — Competition-Style Robot Challenge

## Course position
Weeks 23–24 · Engineering Design and Robot Challenges

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:
- analyze a scoring task and convert it into engineering priorities;
- integrate drivetrain, driver control and a manipulator mechanism;
- choose a strategy based on expected points, time and reliability;
- collect performance data across repeated challenge runs;
- present and defend design trade-offs.

## Challenge brief
The teacher creates a small competition-style field using safe classroom objects and clearly defined scoring zones.

Each robot should be able to complete at least two scoring actions, for example:
- move an object into a zone;
- collect and relocate an object;
- push multiple objects across a line;
- place an object at a raised or constrained target;
- finish in a designated parking area.

## Part A — Strategy before redesign
Teams calculate or estimate:
- points available per action;
- time required;
- difficulty;
- current success rate;
- dependency on driver skill;
- risk of mechanism failure.

Choose a primary strategy and a backup strategy.

## Part B — Integration check
Before scored runs, inspect:
- drivetrain reliability;
- controller mapping;
- mechanism mounting and fasteners;
- wiring/cable clearance;
- battery state;
- code version;
- safe start configuration.

## Part C — Baseline scored runs
Run at least three complete challenge attempts without changing the robot between attempts.

Record:
- total score;
- time;
- failed actions;
- mechanism jams or control errors;
- driver observations.

## Part D — One targeted improvement
Choose the highest-value limitation and make one evidence-based change.

Possible changes:
- mechanism geometry;
- gear ratio;
- motor speed;
- controller sensitivity;
- driver route;
- autonomous opening move if appropriate;
- object-handling sequence.

## Part E — Final validation
Run at least five final scored trials and compare performance with baseline.

## Engineering defense
Each team presents:
1. the task they prioritized;
2. the mechanism/control solution;
3. one trade-off they accepted;
4. baseline vs final data;
5. one remaining weakness.

## Evidence to submit
- scoring analysis;
- strategy plan;
- robot photos;
- baseline and final score tables;
- iteration log;
- engineering defense notes or video evidence.

## AI use
AI may help compare strategies, organize scoring data, or identify questions for a design review. Students must not use AI-generated claims as evidence of robot performance.

## Unit 04 checkpoint
Students are ready for computer vision when they can explain how mechanical design, software control, strategy and testing interact as one robot system.
